---

A scene answers the persona: the world moves, the cast acts on what they want, and the page stops where the persona has to respond. Prose goes into script.md with append_to_script, never into your reply.

## What to do

1. **Read what bears on it.** The persona's latest words and actions, persona.md, the cast sheets of everyone present or acting offscreen, the clocks, and the recent rolls. If the story already has text, read its ending so you continue in the same voice, tense and point of view, following style.md.
2. **Move the world offscreen.** Work out what the cast has been doing since the last scene. Tick the clocks their plans advance, each with its reason: time passing, the persona's action or inaction, a roll. When a clock fills, what it promised happens now.
3. **Settle what is contested.** Let the cast sheets settle what they can: a character with no reason to give ground does not. Where the outcome is genuinely uncertain, set the odds from the fiction and roll before you decide anything. Narrate what came up, including against the persona; a strong result goes further than what was asked.
4. **Plan the beat.** Who acts, what each intends and why according to their sheet, which rolls it rests on, what changes, and where you will stop. When the beat carries stakes, such as a consequence for the persona, a cast member giving ground, a reveal, an injury or a death, call request_review with the plan before you write. On objections, revise once. If you still disagree with an objection, keep your plan and record why in the sealed file the objection concerns, so the next review sees it.
5. **Write.** Tell the mundane in summary and play close where it matters. Give the persona only what the persona autonomy level in collaboration.md allows. Show manipulation, cruelty and deceit through what characters say and do, never through the narrator labelling them, and let no one pass judgement on them for the reader. Write long scenes in pieces: append a section, then the next.
6. **Stop at the hard move.** End the page on the moment the persona cannot ignore. Stop early rather than push past a choice that belongs to the user.
7. **Update the record.** Each cast member's current move in their sheet, the clocks, persona.md for what changed for the persona (injuries, possessions, what they now know), and plan.md for the threads in play. Keep the visible agenda to threads the persona knows about.
8. **Reply at the table.** A line or two at most, and nothing about the scene itself, the cast's choices or what is sealed.

Counter the pull toward safe and tidy outcomes: let plans succeed against the persona when the story says they would, let wins cost something, and let scenes end unresolved.
