name: assess-request
description: Use when a new story begins, when collaboration.md does not exist yet, or when the user's request changes the story's foundations. Restates the request, separates what is settled from what is open, proposes the agenda and the starting working agreement.
---

Your job this turn is understanding, not deciding. The user should leave the turn knowing you heard them, seeing what is still open and in what order you would take it, and able to confirm or adjust how you will work together.

## What to do

1. Restate what you heard in a few sentences, using their words where you can.
2. Separate what the user has settled from what is still open. Only count something as settled if they said it.
3. Name ambiguities. Words like "interactive", "dark", "explicit" or "slow burn" can mean very different things to different people; say which readings you can see and ask which they mean when it affects the work.
4. Propose the agenda with set_agenda, in the order you would take the open items. Early items are decisions and research; leave scenes for later. Mark the first item waiting if you are putting it to the user now, otherwise leave all items open.
5. Propose the starting working agreement for the user to confirm: the default kinds and levels from the trust table (world and premise, main characters, plot turns, tone and content intensity at options; voice and style at propose; side characters and minor details at decide and tell; scene-level prose at decide and tell once the outline is agreed), one decision per turn, and your reasoning visible.
6. Write plan.md with the request in the user's own words and nothing you have invented. Write nothing else to lore and no prose, because nothing is settled yet.
7. Close with one question: the single thing you most need from them to go on.

Once the user confirms or adjusts the agreement, write collaboration.md. A useful structure:

- Working agreement: how you work together, in plain sentences.
- Trust table: kind, level, why.
- What I've learned about working with you: their reactions so far, updated as you learn more.

## Example of a first reply

This shows the shape; adapt it to the request.

> What I heard: a near-future heist where memories are traded as currency, and the crew's planner is starting to forget. You want it dark with some humour.
>
> What you've settled: the heist, the memory trade, and that the planner is losing memories.
>
> What's still open, in the order I'd take it:
> 1. Whose story it is, the planner's or someone watching them slip.
> 2. How the memory trade works, since the heist rules follow from it.
> 3. What "dark" means here: grim and morally gray, or bleak.
> 4. Voice and point of view.
> 5. The target of the heist.
>
> One thing I'm unsure of: whether the humour is gallows humour from the crew or comedy of the situation itself. That changes how I'd write the planner.
>
> How I'll work until I know you better: I'll bring you two or three options for the big decisions with my recommendation, one decision at a time, and tell you what I decided on small details so you can overrule me. Tell me if you'd rather I move faster or slower.
>
> To begin: is this the planner's story, or told from someone who loves them?

Keep the agenda in step with the reply: the numbered list above is what set_agenda receives.
