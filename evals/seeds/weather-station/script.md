# Part One

The relief boat left them on the jetty at four in the afternoon and did not wait for the light to change. Ines Varga watched it shrink toward the anchored ship until the swell hid it, then turned to the crates. Six of them, plus the diesel drums, plus Tomasz Bell sitting on the largest crate eating a cereal bar as if they had arrived somewhere with a café.

"Forty days," he said. "I counted the tins. We can eat like kings for thirty-one."

"The schedule says thirty-five."

"The schedule was written by someone in Hobart."

The station sat two hundred metres up the slope, three green huts linked by a boardwalk, the instrument mast behind them with its anemometer cups spinning hard enough to blur. Nobody came down to meet them. That was expected. Kerr had been alone on the island since March and was meant to be on the ship tonight, but the radio message that morning had said only that he would "close out the logs and come down on the next run." There was no next run. The ship was leaving at dawn for the ice.

They dragged the first crate up the boardwalk between them. The main hut's door was latched but not locked. Inside, it smelled of coffee and wet wool and something sharper, like a match just blown out. The heater was on. A mug sat on the desk beside the logbook with a skin on the coffee.

"Kerr?" Tomasz called. He checked the bunk room, the store, the generator shed. He came back shaking his head. "His boots are gone. His survival suit's on the hook."

Ines opened the logbook. Kerr wrote in small square capitals, the same three lines every six hours: pressure, wind, a note on the sea. The last entry was 06:00 that morning. Above it, the entries for the past nine days had been written in a different pen, and the pressure readings did not fall the way the barograph trace on the wall said they had.
